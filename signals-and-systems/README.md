# Signals and Systems

> Foundations of signal theory and Linear Time-Invariant systems — analysis in both the time and frequency domains using the Discrete Fourier and Z transforms.

**Grade:** 11/20 · **ECTS:** 6 · **Year:** 2 · **Institution:** Universidade Lusófona

---

## Overview

The mathematical groundwork underneath digital signal processing: how signals are represented, how LTI systems act on them, and why moving between time and frequency domains makes otherwise hard problems tractable.

## Topics studied

**Signals**
- Continuous and discrete-time representations
- Signal classification and elementary operations

**LTI systems**
- Linearity and time-invariance, and what those properties buy you
- Impulse response as a complete system characterisation
- **Convolution** — the time-domain description of an LTI system's action

**Transform-domain analysis**
- **Discrete Fourier Transform** — decomposing a signal into frequency components
- **Z Transform** — analysis of discrete-time systems, poles and zeros, stability

## Key takeaways

- **Convolution in time is multiplication in frequency.** This single duality is why frequency-domain analysis is worth the change of representation — an expensive operation becomes a cheap one.
- **The impulse response fully determines an LTI system.** Once you have it, the response to *any* input follows.
- **The Z transform makes stability a geometric property** — pole locations relative to the unit circle — rather than something you reason about case by case.
