# Artificial Intelligence

> Foundations of AI across both traditions — symbolic (knowledge representation and reasoning) and connectionist (neural networks and deep learning).

**Grade:** 16/20 · **ECTS:** 5 · **Year:** 3 · **Institution:** Universidade Lusófona

---

## Overview

The course covered AI as a field with two distinct intellectual lineages rather than as a single set of techniques, which makes the trade-offs between them legible.

## Topics studied

**Foundations and philosophy**
- The Turing Test and what it does and does not claim to measure
- What "intelligence" means operationally in a machine context

**Symbolic AI**
- Knowledge representation
- Automated reasoning and inference
- Rule-based systems

**Connectionist AI**
- Neural network fundamentals
- Deep learning architectures
- Machine learning as statistical function approximation

## Key takeaways

- **The two paradigms answer different questions.** Symbolic systems can explain *why* they concluded something; connectionist systems generalise from data that resists formal specification. Neither subsumes the other.
- **Explainability is a structural property, not a feature you add.** It follows directly from whether the system reasons over explicit representations.
- **The Turing Test is a behavioural criterion**, which is precisely its strength and its limitation — it deliberately declines to make claims about internal states.
